## Slide 7

Here is the shop-floor formula, and it is the same one on the card you test from: Load Angle Factor equals leg length over vertical height. The leg length is the actual slant of the sling, measured pick point to hook. The height is the straight-up headroom from the load to the hook. Slant over straight-up. Run the example: a twenty foot leg with fifteen feet of headroom is twenty over fifteen, which is one point three three. Every leg feels a third more than its share. Notice the factor is always one or bigger, because the slant is always at least as long as the straight-up -- if you ever compute less than one, you flipped it or measured the wrong length. This is the number you can get with a tape measure and no calculator, which is exactly why the trade uses it. ## Slide 9

Let us connect the tape measure to the calculator once, so the formula stops feeling like a trick. Our Load Angle Factor is the flip of that, leg over height, so the factor is one divided by the sine of the angle. Same number, two roads to it. The D-over-d ratio is about how hard the rope has to bend, and both letters are diameters.
