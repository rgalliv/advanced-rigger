Load Sharing and Static Indeterminacy

What Load Sharing Means

Load sharing is how the total weight of a load is divided among the several pick points holding it -- two legs, three, four, or two cranes.

The key question this module answers: how much does each point actually carry? It is rarely as even as it looks. Each leg also still gets its own sling angle factor.

The Two-Legs-Carry Rule

With more than two legs and a rigid load, it is possible for two legs to take practically the full load while the others only steady and balance it.

A rigid load can rock onto a diagonal pair. You cannot count on all the legs sharing.

Rigid vs Equalizing Arrangements

Rigid Means Indeterminate

A rigid arrangement -- fixed-length legs on a stiff load -- has a load share that is indeterminate: you cannot cleanly calculate what each leg carries, and two legs may take most of it.

Every leg still gets its sling angle factor on top of the share uncertainty.

Equalizing Arrangements

An equalizing arrangement lets the legs move relative to each other so they can settle into a more even share -- classically over an equalizer sheave or through an equalizer block.

Movement is the key. Rigid cannot equalize; a sheave or pivot can feed rope until the sides balance.

Spreader and Lifting/Equalizer Beams

The Spreader Beam: Compression

A spreader beam is a compression member. Slings run from the hook down to the two ends of the beam, pushing the ends inward -- so the beam is squeezed along its length.

It is built to resist being crushed end-to-end.

Why Use a Spreader

A spreader beam is used to hold the pick points apart and keep the sling legs vertical.

  • Keeps legs from pulling in at a wide angle (which raises tension and squeezes the load)
  • Supports long loads -- generally over about 12 ft -- against tipping, sliding, or bending

The Lifting (Equalizer) Beam: Bending

A lifting beam -- also called an equalizer beam -- has a single pick point on top and the load hung from points along the bottom. It carries its load in bending.

Opposite of the spreader's compression. An equalizer can pivot at the top pick to share load between two points.

Choosing a Lifting Beam

Choose a lifting beam over a spreader when:

  • Headroom is tight -- a single top pick needs no tall triangle of angled slings
  • You want to place and equalize the bottom pick points precisely

A spreader needs vertical height for its slings; a lifting beam does not.

Center of Gravity, Geometry, and Verification

The Hook Over the Center of Gravity

For a load to hang level, the hook must be directly over the load's center of gravity -- the balance point.

Hook over the CG, the load hangs flat. Hook off to one side, the load tips until the CG swings under the hook.

The CG Decides the Share

When the CG sits closer to one pick point, that near point carries more of the load, and the far points carry less.

Weight leans toward the balance point. Rate and rig the near points for the extra load.

Unequal Leg Lengths

When the sling legs are not the same length, size the rig to the worst-case, most heavily loaded leg -- the one at the flattest angle, with the smallest height-to-length ratio.

Rigid legs do not equalize. Protect the hardest-working leg, not the average.

Verify the Share -- Never Assume

Never assume the load share is even.

Verify it from the center of gravity and the rigging geometry -- leg lengths, angles, where the CG sits -- then rate and rig each point for its real share plus margin.

Below-the-Hook Devices as Equipment

A below-the-hook lifting device (spreader, lifting, or equalizer beam) must always be:

  • Rated and marked with its own working capacity
  • Inspected each shift for deformation, cracks, and wear

It carries its own rating, sized to its own design -- not the crane's capacity.

Even Equal Legs: Still Design for Two

Even with a rigid load, equal-length legs, and the CG centered, the safe capacity assumption is still that two legs may carry the full load.

Equal legs and a centered CG make even sharing more likely -- but a rigid load can still rock onto a diagonal pair. Design for two.
